# 0004. One verdict vocabulary for claims in any direction

- **Date:** recorded 2026-11-24; the rule is the third evidence rule of the paper's Methods
- **Status:** Accepted

## Context

The statements the paper checks come from both sides of a polarised debate: that LGBT+ people die more in Brazil, and
that the Grupo Gay da Bahia figures are fabricated; that violence rose or fell under a given government. A study that
judged one side with one word and the other side with another would be taking a side.

## Decision

A single verdict vocabulary for every claim, whatever its direction: "supported", "partial", "contradicted by the
evidence at a given level", "not supported by what was measured" and "not testable". The word "false" is not used for
a claim about motivation or mortality. Absence in a search is written as "not located in the enumerated search space",
with the space declared.

## Alternatives (reconstructed)

- **True or false per claim.** Rejected: most claims about motivation and mortality cannot be tested with the data
  that exist, and "false" would assert what the data cannot show.

## Consequences

- "Not testable" is a frequent answer, and it is reported as an answer, not as a gap to fill.
- Table 2 of the paper states, for each of the 25 statements, what the data show and what they do not show.
